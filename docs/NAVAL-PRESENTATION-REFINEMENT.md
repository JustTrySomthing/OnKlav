# Naval presentation refinement and purple game loader

The shared HTML export shell now uses the OnKlav logo purple, #603D77, for determinate and indeterminate progress in Chromium/WebKit and Firefox. The canonical CSS lives in developer/games/templates/shared/loading.css; loading.py embeds it in engine-template.zip. Future Web engine rebuilds apply the same theme. The updated SDK archive carries that template and CSS. All bundled games share the rebuilt shell. JS, WASM and audio worklet bytes are unchanged; the export-template checksum is updated honestly. The game engine ABI, controls contract and host minimum are unchanged.

Naval uses a responsive navy workspace with neutral Rotate/Random controls, orange Deploy Fleet and a narrower raised orange Fire button. Removed the top turn caption and deployment/firing helper lines. Place Your Ships labels the higher reserve dock; Enemy Fleet and Your Fleet sit farther below the HUD. The board leaves a dedicated report area above it, while Choose target sits midway between the board and Fire.

Combat reports are unboxed, readable phrases: You missed, Direct hit, Enemy ship destroyed, Enemy missed, Your ship was hit and Your ship was destroyed. They pop in with a damped scale motion, brief hit shake and soft text shadow. Reduced motion keeps scale/position stationary. Results hold for3.4 seconds on a miss,4.1 on a hit and4.8 on a sunk ship, then allow a short view transition. Queued friend shots stay ordered behind the active report. The defending fleet remains visible for the incoming impact/result before returning to targeting. Transport waits and fleet-deployment waits remain explicit.

The shell arc is now proportional to travel distance, capped at36 CSS-game pixels, rather than rising52% of the board height. Its0.95-second flight includes the original cannon cue and a distinct air rush/descending whistle before impact. Original hit/miss/sink cues remain. The new original Fleet Command score adds a nautical minor-key melody, bass, pads, arpeggios, ocean air and percussion; its player gain is-9dB, substantially louder than the old quiet shared sequence. Music/effects retain the shared buses, master mute, preferences and pause lifecycle. No new runtime library or permission is introduced.
